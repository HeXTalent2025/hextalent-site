Context

What I've heard about Aurora Dairies

A brief written recap so you can sense-check my understanding before I start representing the business in candidate conversations.

  • A longstanding Australian dairy business with a distributed operational footprint across Victoria and Tasmania, plus a Melbourne CBD head office and regional offices.
  • Currently in a standalone chapter following the transition out of asset-management structure, roughly a year in.
  • Currently between CEOs, with executive responsibilities shared across three senior leaders (yourself included). This has created significant autonomy for HR to move at pace on what matters.
  • Data uplift is the number-one strategic focus across the business, led by the new Chief Technical Services Officer. HR data uplift sits as a parallel priority within your function.
  • Workforce: approximately 180 permanent, 140 casual with seasonal uplift, and ~50 corporate roles (with ~40 in Melbourne HQ).
  • HR function is deliberately early in its standalone maturity — canvas across strategy, performance framework, L&D, EVP, and benefits.
  • Culture: "a longstanding business with a start-up vibe" — get-shit-done mentality, high cross-functional collaboration, blame-free environment for good-faith failures.
If any of the above doesn't feel right, let me know and I'll adjust before it hardens into how I represent the business externally.

Role 1 · HRBP
Role 1

HR Business Partner

What this role is designed to do

Enable you to be strategic by taking meaningful ownership of operational HR delivery, project execution, and employee relations casework. The role sits as a senior peer to the regional HR Advisors — not managing them, but supporting and lifting the function as a whole.

Where the emphasis sits (my read)

  • Operational HR: Moderate — corporate team plus support to regional advisors.
  • Employee relations: Meaningful — investigations, letter drafting, coaching leaders. Under your oversight but with real ownership of caseload.
  • Project delivery: Significant — performance framework rollout, HR system review, L&D framework build, workforce accommodation programs, process automation.
  • Strategic contribution: Emerging — supporting you to shape and deliver the three-year HR vision.
  • Recruitment: Light — regional advisors own their regions; some corporate hiring only.

Candidate profile priorities

The person you're looking for brings:

  • Strong HR generalist grounding with a real project delivery track record.
  • Solid ER depth — the confidence to run investigations and coach managers under your oversight.
  • Get-things-done mentality with genuine energy for a build environment.
  • Attitude first — willingness to step outside a rigid PD when the business needs it.
  • Openness to travel — willing to jump in the car at 6am when it matters.
  • Long-term commitment — someone who wants to stay and build.

Preferred: exposure to operational or distributed-workforce environments (any industry — dairy, agriculture, FMCG, manufacturing, logistics, resources, energy, healthcare operations).

12-month "great hire" signal

  • You're materially less in the weeds.
  • HR maturity has advanced measurably.
  • At least one substantial project meaningfully delivered.
  • Regional advisors feel supported, not competed with.

Role 2 · HR Analyst & Systems Administrator
Role 2

HR Analyst & Systems Administrator

What this role is designed to do

Bring genuine workforce analytics capability to the HR function, own the HRIS environment, and provide the data foundation to move HR decisions from "we think this is happening" to "we know this is happening."

Where the emphasis sits (my read)

  • Workforce analytics & reporting: ~40% — dashboards, KPIs, ad-hoc leader reports, trend analysis.
  • HRIS administration: ~30% — ELMO administration, workflows, first-line support, contributing to future system decisions.
  • Process improvement & project support: ~15% — UAT, automation opportunities.
  • Data management & governance: ~10% — data quality audits, privacy.
  • Some HR admin: ~5% — corporate contracts only.

Candidate profile priorities

The person you're looking for brings:

  • Analytical mindset first — can produce insight, not just reports.
  • Advanced Excel as the non-negotiable technical foundation.
  • HRIS pattern recognition — has worked in any HRIS platform (technology-agnostic hiring).
  • Curiosity about workforce data and comfort in a build environment (not a maintenance seat).
  • Long-term mindset — this role is designed to grow with the business.

Preferred: Power BI, SQL, Power Query, Power Automate, or experience implementing/upgrading HR systems. Backgrounds welcome include HR Analyst, HRIS Administrator, People & Culture Analyst, Data Analyst with HR crossover, or HR Admin with genuine analytical capability.

Timing sensitivity

The incumbent's leave window (25 July → 25 August) gives us a natural runway. The goal is a strong replacement signed and ready to start before the incumbent returns from leave. That shapes my sourcing pace and shortlist cadence for this role.

How I'll run both searches discreetly

Given the sensitivity you've flagged (particularly around the HR Analyst role and the current incumbent), both searches will run under a strict confidentiality protocol.

External representation

  • All advertising (LinkedIn, SEEK, Careers in HR) will run under HeXTalent branding — no mention of Aurora Dairies, no industry-identifying language, no state/region specifics beyond Melbourne CBD.
  • Ad copy has been carefully written to describe the opportunity without identifying the business (I've screened it against a specific list of identifying details — dairy, farms, workforce numbers, states, regional office locations, system names — none appear in the ad body).
  • Client name is only shared with candidates who have progressed past initial screening and committed to confidentiality.
  • The word track for the "why is the role being recruited now?" question refers to "potential restructures in the HR team that have yet to be communicated internally" — matches the language you suggested.

Candidate commitments

Every candidate I engage will be asked to:

  • Keep the client name confidential to themselves.
  • Not canvass any current Aurora Dairies employees for information.
  • Direct all questions to me rather than trying to source information independently.

CEO context — how I'll handle it

Candidates will ask. My approach is honest but restrained: acknowledge the recent CEO departure and the current shared-leadership structure, and reframe the vacuum as the source of the current autonomy. I won't speculate about the CEO's departure beyond what's necessary.
