## Design decisions

### Consistency between the two posts
Both posts follow the same structure so your feed reads as a coherent campaign:
1. **Callback opener** — "The [role] I've been talking about is now live on [platform]" — rewards the audience who's been following the campaign, orients newcomers
2. **Fact line** in bold — location, employment type, salary
3. **Company/culture context** — same anonymised framing across both
4. **"The two cards below..." bridge** — points the reader to the attached visuals
5. **Ideal-candidate hook** — the emotional pitch (leave a mark / produce insight)
6. **Link + fallback CTA** (DM/email)
7. **Referral invitation** — expands reach into people's networks
8. **Hashtags** — different tags per role (HRBP vs HRAnalytics/HRIS)

### What's different between the two
- **Hook line** (leave a mark vs produce insight) — pulled directly from the ICP cards for each role, giving each post its own identity
- **Culture framing** (build-mode HR generally vs analytics-led seat specifically) — sharpens each post to the right candidate audience
- **Hashtags** — HRBP audience vs HR Analytics/HRIS audience have different tag habits on LinkedIn

### Suggested cadence
- Post the **HRBP post first** (higher-volume audience, warmer market)
- Wait **2–3 days**, then post the **HR Analyst post**
- Spaced posts keep the campaign visible in the feed for ~a week without overwhelming your network

### One optional tweak
If you want a slightly warmer close on either post, replace *"Referrals from your network genuinely welcomed."* with *"Or tag someone in the comments who fits — I'll take it from there."* — that variant tends to drive slightly more comment engagement (which LinkedIn's algorithm rewards).
