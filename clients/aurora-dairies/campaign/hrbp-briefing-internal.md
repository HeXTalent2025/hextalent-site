## 2. Client Context — Aurora Dairies

### Business
- Longstanding Australian dairy operations business
- Operates multiple farms across **Northern Victoria**, **Gippsland**, **Warrnambool region**, and **Tasmania**
- Each farm treated as an individual business unit with its own P&L, milking platform, and nuances
- Farms operate on **three different dairy software systems** — a known data-integrity headache
- Some farms calve once a year (spring OR autumn), some do split calving
- Seasonal casual uplift during calving windows

### Ownership & Structural History
- Announced July 2024 that they would transition from asset-manager ownership to become a **standalone business**
- Transition took ~12 months; standalone from **1 August 2025**
- Previous CEO was an employee of the asset manager and did not transfer across
- Standalone-era CEO was appointed early 2026, joined late March 2026, **resigned late June 2026** (after ~3 months)
- No public information on why the CEO left. Jenna's read: he came from FMCG, was used to established processes, and found the "expected to be hands-on in a start-up-style operation" reality overwhelming
- **Currently no CEO** — CEO responsibilities are being split across three people, including Jenna
- This vacuum has actually created considerable autonomy, and this is a selling point (see §7)

### Strategic Priorities (Business-wide)
- **DATA is the number-one focus.** A new Chief Technical Services Officer has been appointed to get data integrity across farms sorted
- Broader business goals depend on data uplift: herd optimisation (breed, semen quality, feed programs), production planning, cost-per-litre analytics
- No formalised HR strategy yet — but a clear "get shit done" direction from Jenna and the top team

### Culture
- **"Longstanding business with a start-up vibe"** — Jenna's exact framing
- Get-shit-done mentality; high autonomy; blame-free environment for good-faith failures
- Cross-collaboration between head office and farms is strong ("phenomenal" per Jenna)
- Everyone is very new to the standalone business — most team members recruited in last 12 months
- Some legacy employees remain who are "mates of managers" from the asset-management era; not many, and Jenna has done a significant clean-up

## 3. HR Function Context

### Structure
- **Jenna Tran** — GM HR (Melbourne HQ)
- **HR Advisors** based regionally:
  - Warrnambool (also covers Mount Gambier)
  - Gippsland
  - Tasmania
- **Safety Advisors** in the same regions (separate WHS function but co-located)
- **HR Administrator** (Melbourne) — full-time, being replaced (see §9 confidentiality)
- **Regional admin support** in Mount Gambier and Gippsland
- ~50 corporate roles in total; ~40 in Melbourne HQ
- **~180 permanent employees** business-wide, **~140 casuals**, uplift in peak seasons

### Systems
- **HRIS is ELMO** — originally an LMS that evolved into a HRIS. **Not fit for purpose.** Jenna intends to build a business case to change systems (currently deprioritised due to CEO exit)
- Configuration workarounds and out-of-system processes are common
- Culture Amp is **configured and ready to go**, not yet launched
- Payroll runs on the same system; payroll team is overwhelmed with queries every fortnightly cycle
- Performance module in ELMO is "very rigid and not fit for purpose" — no formalised performance review process currently

### HR Maturity
- **Deliberately early-stage.** Building rhythms, processes, systems, strategy from a low base
- **L&D is a gap.** WHS team has a practical LMS ("how to put a cup on a cow") but no broader L&D framework. There is a proposal from a consultant sitting on hold
- **Performance management is not structured.** KPIs and a process are on Jenna's roadmap
- **Recruitment:** Regional advisors handle their own regions. Head office recruitment has been leaning on agencies. Not a big volume component of the role
- **On-farm accommodation** provided to some staff is a known issue — inconsistencies, questionable housing quality, no reporting mechanism, everything on spreadsheets. Ripe project for the HRBP to solve

### Employee Relations
- ER cases come in waves — some months are quiet, others intense
- Volume is moderating as regional advisors get better at screening at hire
- Some cases are "out of this world" per Jenna — the demographics and settings are different from metropolitan white-collar work
- "If you hurt an animal, you're out" — clear standard, but process needs to be waterproof
- Jenna currently gets pulled into ER cases; this role needs to relieve her while keeping her oversight

## 4. The Role in Detail

### Purpose
Enable Jenna to be strategic by taking meaningful ownership of operational HR, project delivery, and ER support. This role is the **operational backbone + project engine** of the HR function, sitting alongside (but not above) the regional HR Advisors.

### Balance
- **Operational HR:** Moderate — supporting Jenna, backing up regional advisors, corporate HR for the ~50 head-office team
- **ER Support:** Meaningful — running investigations and drafting letters under Jenna's oversight, not owning outright
- **Project Delivery:** Significant — housing standards, performance framework, L&D rollout, system configuration, EVP work, remuneration & benefits review
- **Strategic Contribution:** Emerging — Jenna has a 3-year vision; this person will help build the strategy and delivery plan alongside her
- **Recruitment:** Minimal — regional advisors run their own regions; some corporate hiring as it arises

### Key Responsibilities (from PD)
- Operational HR advice across the employee lifecycle
- ER: performance, conduct, grievances, investigations
- Compliance with employment legislation and industrial instruments
- Develop and maintain policies, procedures, templates
- Lead HR projects end-to-end
- Support workforce change and consultation processes
- Process improvement and automation opportunities
- Coach leaders; commercial HR advice
- Workforce data analysis and reporting
- Budgeting and establishment management

### Success Profile
- **First 30 days:** Immerse in business, get across HR maturity picture, meet regional team
- **First 60 days:** Take real ownership of one or two projects, start relieving Jenna on live ER cases
- **First 90 days & beyond:** Jenna is flexible on 90-day mapping — expects the incoming person to shape it based on capability and observations
- **12-month "great hire":** Jenna is materially less "in the weeds", HR maturity has advanced measurably, at least one significant project (housing, performance framework, or L&D rollout) meaningfully delivered

### Reporting Line & Team Dynamics
- Reports directly to Jenna
- **Standalone role — NOT managing the regional HR Advisors.** More of a senior peer who supports them
- Key internal partners: Jenna, regional HR Advisors, WHS advisors, Payroll, Finance, IT, General Managers (farm & operations)

- Jenna's own HR team (regional advisors, HR Admin) **do not know**

When candidates ask "why the recruitment now / what's the context internally?":

There are potential restructures across the HR team that have yet to be communicated internally, and this HRBP role is one of the new roles being created to support Jenna and the growing function. All candidates are asked to keep the recruitment fully confidential and to not canvass anyone else at the organisation for insights — Jenna is the sole source of internal information for the search."

### Explicit ask of every candidate
- Do not DM regional HR Advisors or the HR Admin on LinkedIn asking about the business
- Do not mention Aurora Dairies or the search to their current network in a way that could get back to the business
- Come to me (Jeff) with any questions rather than trying to source information independently

### CEO context — how to handle
- Candidates will ask. It's the elephant in the room
- **What to say:** "The prior CEO left in late June after ~3 months in the role. Aurora Dairies is a longstanding dairy business that transitioned to standalone operations in August 2025. The CEO seat is currently split across three interim leaders including Jenna, which is actually creating unusually good autonomy across the HR function right now."
- **What NOT to say:** Anything about the FMCG-background theory, or that "he found being hands-on overwhelming." That's Jenna's private read
- Frame the vacuum as opportunity: "This is a rare period where HR can make a real mark on how the business runs going forward, before a new CEO comes in with their own preferences."
